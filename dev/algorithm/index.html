How it works

This page explains the two kernels behind sig and its gradient: the Horner update used in the forward pass, and the reversible backward pass.

Setting. A path $x_1, \dots, x_N \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is treated as piecewise linear, with increments $z_j = x_{j+1} - x_j$, $j = 1, \dots, n$, $n = N - 1$. Signatures live in the truncated tensor algebra $T^{(M)}(\mathbb{R}^D) = \bigoplus_{k=0}^M (\mathbb{R}^D)^{\otimes k}$. $S_k$ denotes the level-$k$ component of $S$. Level-$k$ coefficients are indexed by words $w = w_1 \dots w_k$ over $\{1, \dots, D\}$, and in memory the last letter varies fastest.

The Horner update

The signature of one linear segment with increment $z$ is the truncated exponential $\exp(z) = \sum_{l=0}^M z^{\otimes l} / l!$. Chen's identity glues segments together: $S_j = S_{j-1} \otimes \exp(z_j)$, with $S_0 = 1$. Level $k$ of the product is

\[(S \otimes \exp z)_k = \sum_{i=0}^k S_i \otimes \frac{z^{\otimes (k-i)}}{(k-i)!} = S_k + S_{k-1} \otimes z + S_{k-2} \otimes \frac{z \otimes z}{2!} + \dots + \frac{z^{\otimes k}}{k!}.\]

Read this as a polynomial in $z$, where "multiply by $z$" always means appending $z$ on the right. Horner's rule factors out one $\otimes z$ at a time:

\[(S \otimes \exp z)_k = S_k + \Big( S_{k-1} + \Big( S_{k-2} + \Big( \cdots \Big( S_1 + \frac{z}{k} \Big) \otimes \frac{z}{k-1} \cdots \Big) \otimes \frac{z}{2} \Big) \Big) \otimes z .\]

Following $S_i$ outwards, it collects the factors $\frac{z}{k-i} \cdot \frac{z}{k-i-1} \cdots \frac{z}{1} = z^{\otimes (k-i)} / (k-i)!$, as required. As a recursion, for each $k = M, \dots, 2$:

\[v_1 = \frac{z}{k}, \qquad u_i = v_i + S_i, \qquad v_{i+1} = c_i \, u_i \otimes z, \qquad c_i = \frac{1}{k-i}, \qquad i = 1, \dots, k-1, \qquad S_k \mathrel{+}= v_k ,\]

followed by $S_1 \mathrel{+}= z$.

  • Cost. $v_{i+1}$ has $D^{i+1}$ entries, so chain $k$ costs about $D^k$ multiply-adds, and a whole step about $D^M (D/(D-1))^2$: $O(N D^M)$ for the path. Forming each term $S_i \otimes z^{\otimes(k-i)}$ separately costs about $k D^k$ per level, so Horner saves a factor of about $M$ and never forms $\exp(z)$.
  • In place. Level $k$ only reads $S_1, \dots, S_{k-1}$, which must still hold their old values. Updating levels from $k = M$ down to $1$ guarantees this, so no copy of $S$ is needed.

The reversible backward pass

The forward pass is a chain of maps $S_j = \Phi_{z_j}(S_{j-1})$ with $\Phi_z(S) = S \otimes \exp(z)$, and the loss is $L(S_n)$. With $G_j = \partial L / \partial S_j$, the chain rule gives

\[G_{j-1} = \Big(\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial S}\Big)^{\!T} G_j, \qquad \bar z_j = \Big(\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial z}\Big)^{\!T} G_j ,\]

both evaluated at $(S_{j-1}, z_j)$. Generic reverse-mode AD stores every intermediate signature, at a cost of $O(N D^M)$ memory.

Rebuild instead of storing

Signatures form a group: $\exp(z) \otimes \exp(-z) = 1$, also after truncation. Hence

\[S_{j-1} = S_j \otimes \exp(-z_j) = \Phi_{-z_j}(S_j),\]

the same Horner update with $-z$. Walking backwards from $S_n$, each earlier signature is rebuilt just in time. Only one signature and one cotangent are alive at once, so memory is $O(D^M)$, independent of $N$.

Transposing the Horner chain

View a level-$(i+1)$ tensor as a $D^i \times D$ matrix: rows are indexed by the first $i$ letters of the word, columns by the last letter. A chain step is then an outer product, $V_{i+1} = c_i \, u_i \, z^T$, and its transposes are matrix–vector products:

\[\bar u_i = c_i \, \bar V_{i+1} \, z \quad \text{(to the prefix)}, \qquad \bar z \mathrel{+}= c_i \, \bar V_{i+1}^T \, u_i \quad \text{(to the increment)}.\]

Since $u_i = v_i + S_i$, the same $\bar u_i$ is added to $G_i$ and passed down as $\bar v_i$. Chain $k$ starts from $\bar V_k = G_k$; its innermost step $v_1 = z/k$ contributes $\bar v_1 / k$ to $\bar z$, and the level-1 update contributes $G_1$.

For one path step, with $S = S_{j-1}$ already rebuilt and $G = G_j$:

  1. $\bar z \leftarrow G_1$.
  2. For $k = 2, \dots, M$ (increasing):
    1. recompute $u_1, \dots, u_{k-1}$ of chain $k$ from $S$ and $z$;
    2. for $i = k-1, \dots, 1$: $\bar u_i = c_i \bar V_{i+1} z$, $\bar z \mathrel{+}= c_i \bar V_{i+1}^T u_i$, $G_i \mathrel{+}= \bar u_i$, $\bar v_i = \bar u_i$;
    3. $\bar z \mathrel{+}= \bar v_1 / k$.
  3. On exit $G = G_{j-1}$. Finally $\bar x_{j+1} \mathrel{+}= \bar z$ and $\bar x_j \mathrel{-}= \bar z$, because $z_j = x_{j+1} - x_j$.

Chain $k$ reads $G_k$ and only writes to levels below $k$, and level $k$ receives contributions only from chains above $k$. Processing $k$ in increasing order (the mirror of the forward pass) therefore lets $G$ be updated in place.

Cost and accuracy

Per path step: about one Horner update to rebuild $S_{j-1}$, one to recompute the $u_i$, and about two for the transposed chain, so roughly four forward steps in total. The hot loop does not allocate.

The price of rebuilding is rounding drift: each $\otimes \exp(-z)$ adds a little error, growing with $N$ and the size of the increments. In practice it is small: rewinding a 10 000-step path with unit-scale increments ($D = 4$, $M = 5$) returns to the unit tensor within $1.3 \cdot 10^{-10}$.
